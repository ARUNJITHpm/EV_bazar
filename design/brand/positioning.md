# Positioning

## What the business is

Not a CPO. An **independent layer above the CPOs**: assess whether a location is
viable for EV charging, determine which CPO suits that site, and connect the
two. The partnered CPO then delivers charger, installation, CMS and service.

## The one sentence

**We tell you whether your space can make money from EV charging — then match
you with the operator best suited to build and run it.**

- Starts at **the site**, not the hardware — a CPO's own advertising can never
  open this way.
- Implies a real answer, including "no", which is what makes the assessment
  credible.
- "Match you with the operator" signals independence across many CPOs.

## Tagline

*Right site. Right operator.*

## How this differs from a CPO's own advertising

| A CPO says | We say |
|---|---|
| "Our chargers, our network, our app." | "Which operator fits *your* site." |
| Sells one option. | Compares many, recommends one. |
| Starts at the product. | Starts at the location. |

## Per-audience

| Audience | Line |
|---|---|
| RWAs / property owners | Find out what your parking is worth as a charging site — we'll bring you the right operator to run it. |
| Fleets and businesses | We match your depot and duty cycles to the operator who can actually serve them. |
| CPOs | Pre-qualified sites that fit your network — matched to your hardware and economics, not scattershot leads. |

## Notes

- **The bank angle is deliberately out of the core message.** The bank-grade
  site report is a proof point under "why the assessment is credible", not the
  headline and not in the first five seconds.
- The pain being solved is **uncertain ROI**. Every line should imply certainty
  before spend.
- One word needs defining wherever it first appears: a reader who is not an EV
  person does not know what an *operator* is, and will guess "the company that
  sells chargers" — which quietly undoes the independence argument. See
  `copy-pass.md` §1.
